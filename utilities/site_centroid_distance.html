Site Centroid Distance#

uniharmony.metrics.site_centroid_distance measures how far apart the site centroids (per-site mean vectors) are, relative to what sampling noise alone would produce. It is a data-driven measure of the location Effect of Site (EoS) that remains in the data, before or after harmonization.

Quick Start#

from uniharmony.datasets import make_multisite_classification
from uniharmony.metrics import site_centroid_distance

X, y, sites = make_multisite_classification(n_sites=4, n_samples=800)
result = site_centroid_distance(X, sites, y=y)

result["statistic"], result["null_mean"], result["p_value"]
result["pairwise_distance"]  # site x site DataFrame

What is computed#

With site centroids \(\bar x_k\), grand mean \(\bar x\) and \(n_k\) samples per site, the between-site and within-site scatter matrices are

\[ B = \sum_k n_k (\bar x_k - \bar x)(\bar x_k - \bar x)^\top, \qquad W = \sum_k \sum_{i \in k} (x_i - \bar x_k)(x_i - \bar x_k)^\top . \]

The statistic is the pseudo-F

\[ F = \frac{\operatorname{tr}(M B) / (K - 1)}{\operatorname{tr}(M W) / (n - K)}, \]

where \(M\) sets the geometry (metric):

Without EoS, \(E[B] = (K-1)\Sigma\) and \(E[W] = (n-K)\Sigma\), so \(F \approx 1\) for the first two metrics. The exact null is obtained by permuting the site labels, which gives null_mean, z_score and p_value. r2 is the share of the variance explained by site.

Pairwise distances#

The squared distance between two estimated centroids is biased by sampling noise:

\[ E\,\lVert \bar x_a - \bar x_b \rVert_M^2 = \lVert \mu_a - \mu_b \rVert_M^2 + \frac{\operatorname{tr}(M S_a)}{n_a} + \frac{\operatorname{tr}(M S_b)}{n_b} . \]

Small sites therefore look far away even without EoS. pairwise_distance subtracts this term (the unbiased linear-kernel MMD estimator) and clips negative values to 0. With the Mahalanobis metric a distance \(D\) is a multivariate standardized mean difference: for Gaussian sites, the best balanced accuracy of telling the two sites apart is \(\Phi(D/2)\) (0.69 for \(D = 1\), 0.84 for \(D = 2\)).

Good practice#

  • Compute it on held-out data. ComBat-like methods equalize the site means of the data they were fitted on by construction.

  • Pass the biology as y when its distribution differs between sites (e.g. diagnosis). Sites are then compared within each class and the labels are permuted within classes, so biological differences are not counted as EoS. y must be categorical.

  • Compute it in the feature space, not on t-SNE or UMAP embeddings, which do not preserve distances between clusters. PCA projections can be used to show the centroids.

  • It only measures site means. Differences in variance or covariance between sites are not captured; complement it with uniharmony.metrics.evaluate_site_prediction using a flexible model.

  • Cost. The Mahalanobis metric inverts an n_features x n_features matrix per permutation; for many features use "standardized" or fewer permutations.
